What waiting costs

  • Late amounts grow at 5% a month, up to 25%, plus interest, and TAT unpaid more than 60 days picks up another 20%.
  • There's no amnesty. A bill to forgive penalties for owners switching to long-term rentals died in 2024, and nothing replaced it. What's owed stays owed in full.
  • A return you never filed has no statute of limitations. Filing it is what starts the clock.
  • No clearance while returns are missing, and no registration or renewal without a clearance.
  • The state tends to find you first. Rent collectors now report the owners they collect for, and the notice comes to you, not to your manager.

Coming forward on your own schedule beats being found. That's the whole reason to do it this month rather than next.

Questions

The things people actually ask

I got a letter from the state. What do I do?

Don't pay it yet. Most of these letters are estimates rather than findings, and an estimate can be wrong in your favor. Send it over and you'll find out what's actually owed for those periods, get the missing returns filed, and get the notice answered. If a penalty is worth challenging, it gets challenged before anyone pays anything. More on letters here.

What does this cost?

Catch-up work is a flat fee from $500, quoted in writing before anything starts. One property with a couple of missed years is usually $500 to $750. Monthly filing is $49 to $59 per property plus a one-time $249 setup. Property managers pay $39 per owner per month with a four owner minimum and no setup fee. Long-term landlords pay $249 a year per property. All of it on one page.

What happens if I just leave it?

Hawaii has no amnesty program for this and no statute of limitations on a return that was never filed. Late amounts grow 5% a month up to 25%, plus interest. And the state now gets reports from rent collectors naming the owners they collect for, so waiting to be found is not a plan. Coming forward first puts you in a far better position than being found.

Are you a CPA?

No, and we're upfront about it. We're a tax return preparer registered with the Hawaii Department of Taxation as a Verified Practitioner, which is what lets us file your returns and deal with the Department for you. It's a registration, not an endorsement. We're not a CPA firm or a law firm, and we don't prepare income tax returns. Here's the full picture.

I live on the mainland and can't even log in. Can you help?

Yes, and this is one of the most common reasons people call. You don't need your own Hawaii Tax Online login. Once you sign Form N-848, the state power of attorney, access to your accounts gets requested and the filing happens from here. It works the same way if you're outside the United States.

Do you hold my tax money?

Never. When a return is filed, the payment goes from your own bank account straight to the state. It doesn't pass through us, so there's no account of ours for it to sit in or go missing from. You see the amount before it leaves and you get the confirmation number afterwards.

Will you replace my accountant?

No. General excise tax and transient accommodations tax are separate state filings that most income tax preparers don't touch month to month, and that's the slice we handle. Your income taxes stay with whoever does them now, and at tax time you get clean records of everything that was filed for you.

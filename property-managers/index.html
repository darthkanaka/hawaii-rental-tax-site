How it runs, month to month

  1. By the 10th, you send the figures. Gross rent per owner, per property, in whatever spreadsheet you already keep. We'll take your format rather than make you learn ours.
  2. We work out every return that's due for each owner, at each one's own filing frequency, which is rarely the same across a book.
  3. We file and pay by the 20th. State returns through Hawaii Tax Online, county transient accommodations tax through whichever county portal applies. The money moves from each owner's own bank account to the state. It never touches your account and it never touches ours.
  4. You get the confirmations back in one summary: who was filed, for what period, how much left their account, and the confirmation number.

In April we do the annual reconciliations, the G-49 and the TA-2, for every owner on the desk. That's included, not an extra.

What it costs

$39 per owner per month, with a minimum of four owners, so $156 a month at the low end. No setup fee. No annual contract. Billed monthly, cancel with a month's notice.

For comparison, the one other Hawaii specialist that publishes a property manager rate charges $82.50 per owner. We're less than half that because your data arrives clean and on a schedule, which is worth a lot more to us than chasing forty owners individually.

If a new owner comes to you already behind on filings, that's catch-up work and it's quoted separately, flat, from $500. We'd tell you that before starting rather than let it appear on an invoice.

Your clients stay your clients

We're not looking for your owners. We don't do property management, we don't do income tax, and we won't approach anyone you introduce us to about anything other than the filings you've asked us to handle.

If you'd rather your owners never hear our name, that works. Put it on your letterhead and pass on the confirmations as your own reporting. If you'd rather introduce us directly so we can chase the odd missing figure without going through you, that works too. Your call, and it can change later.

The honest part about paperwork

There's one step we can't make disappear. Hawaii requires each taxpayer to sign their own Form N-848, the state power of attorney, before anyone else can file for them. The state's instructions don't mention electronic signatures, so we treat it as a wet signature that gets mailed or faxed.

So each owner signs two things once, at the start: our engagement letter and their N-848. We prepare both, pre-filled, and we chase the returns. What we need from you is the introduction and an accurate list of owners and properties. For a book of ten owners this is an afternoon of signatures spread over a couple of weeks, and then it's done.

One more thing worth knowing up front: we can file and pay on an owner's accounts, but we can't change their details. If someone's filing frequency needs to change, the owner has to submit that themselves on Form BB-1. We'll tell you when it comes up and fill in the form.

Why hand this over at all

Because it's the one job in your business with a hard legal deadline and absolutely no upside. Nobody has ever renewed a management agreement because the G-45 went in on time. But the 20th doesn't move, it comes round twelve times a year, and a missed one costs your owner money and costs you a conversation you don't want to have.

And if you're honest about it, the filings are probably the thing most likely to go wrong when you're short-handed or away.

If we file late, we pay the penalty

If a return we're responsible for goes in late or wrong because of us, we pay the penalty that causes, up to $2,500 per property per calendar year. Conditions apply, including telling us within 10 days and letting us ask the Department to waive it first. Full terms here.

It doesn't cover the tax itself, periods before we started, or anything caused by figures that reached us late or wrong. That last one is worth saying out loud in a manager arrangement: the deadline we can hold is the one we get the data for.

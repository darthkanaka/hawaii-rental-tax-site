Monthly filing

What a month actually looks like

For owners who are current and want to stay that way without thinking about it. It's also where you can land after a catch-up, never a condition of one.

1

By the 10th, your figures reach us

Forward your Airbnb or VRBO payout statement, or set them to come to us automatically. Direct bookings and leases work the same way. If something's missing we'll chase you, not guess.

2

By the 20th, everything's filed and paid

State returns through Hawaii Tax Online, county transient accommodations tax through your county's portal. The payment goes from your own bank account straight to the state. We never hold your tax money.

3

You get a confirmation number every time

Which return, which period, how much left your account, and the state's confirmation number. Keep them or ignore them, but they're yours and they're the proof.

4

April brings the reconciliations

The G-49 and, for short-term rentals, the TA-2 and the county annual. They're included. This is the filing people most often don't know exists, and it's the one that closes a year out.

The on-time guarantee

If a return we're responsible for goes in late or wrong because of us, we pay the penalty that causes, up to $2,500 per property per calendar year. Conditions apply, including telling us within 10 days and letting us ask the Department to waive it first. Full terms here.

It doesn't cover the tax itself, periods before we started working for you, or penalties caused by records that reached us late or wrong.

Canceling

Any time, by email to aloha@hawaiirentaltax.com or by phone. It takes effect at the end of the month you've already paid for, and we finish any filing due in that month. Then the N-848 authorization is withdrawn and your records come back to you, so you or anyone else can pick it up.

Long-term rentals

Renting long-term? You owe GET, and that's it

A long-term rental is exempt from both transient accommodations taxes, but general excise tax still applies to the rent, at about 4.5% on most islands. Plenty of landlords find that out years in.

The filing load is light. Most long-term landlords file semiannually or quarterly, so it's two to four returns a year plus the annual reconciliation. Paying a monthly fee for that would be silly, and $588 a year to file twice would be worse than silly, so you pay $249 a year per property instead. That covers the periodic returns and the G-49, with no setup fee, and it renews each year until you cancel.

If you've got a long-term rental that's never been filed, start with the catch-up and move onto the annual plan once you're current.

The plain-English explainer on GET for long-term rentals covers what's taxable and why it's on gross rent rather than profit.

What you're on the hook for

The three taxes, in one breath

Hawaii is unusual. Airbnb and VRBO don't collect or file any of this for you, and they don't register you either. It all sits with the owner.

~4.5%

General excise tax

On every dollar of rental income, long-term rentals included. It's a tax on gross, not on profit, so your mortgage and repairs don't reduce it.

11%

State transient accommodations tax

On short-term stays, up from 10.25% in January 2026. Long-term rentals don't pay it.

3%

County transient accommodations tax

On top of the state one, in all four counties, and filed on a different portal. This is the one people miss.
